## First-sale test: Recruitment Readiness Review
Working candidate, not launched. The current launch kit proposes £149, a five-working-day turnaround after complete intake, and one vacancy. Treat that price and turnaround as pending validation. Start with this narrow offer before expanding.

**Sample demo, fictional:** A local maintenance firm advertises for a “flexible administrator” but gets applicants who expect remote work. Replace the title and opening with “Maintenance Scheduler | on-site | Mon–Fri, 8 am–4 pm” (fictional hours). State one outcome: keeping the engineer diary current and escalating urgent jobs. Use one interview prompt: “An urgent repair arrives and the only available engineer is due at a routine visit. What would you check before moving the appointment, and who would you tell?” Sample scoring: 0 = moves the visit without checking; 1 = checks urgency or availability but misses the handover; 2 = checks urgency, availability and impact, then communicates and records the change. This is a demonstration of a deliverable, not a customer outcome.

**Suggested public hook after release checks:** “If the vacancy says ‘flexible’ but the job is on-site at 8 am, the wrong people may be reading it. Here’s what we’d put in the first two lines.” Show the before/after and one interview question in original media, then use one verified destination. Publish no checkout CTA until payment and delivery are verified.

**Run the experiment:** Record product, channel, hook, destination, qualified enquiries, completed paid orders, refunds and net receipts. Use a zero-cost checkout/delivery rehearsal where supported and distinguish it from payment processor validation. Count only a genuine paid customer order as first revenue. Reassess hook, destination or fit after the first controlled organic test; make no claims from silence. Any paid test needs a named channel, item, cost ceiling, stop condition and a verified checkout before spend.
