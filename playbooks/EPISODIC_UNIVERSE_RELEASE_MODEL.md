# TA GuruLabs / JingWu Media — Episodic Universe Release Model
Date: 2026-11-19 | Founder direction: release games, films and comics in achievable serial episodes rather than holding entire seasons or games until finished.

## Principle
**Ship a complete, compelling chapter; preserve the whole universe.** An episode must stand on its own, have a beginning/middle/end and an inviting continuation hook. A cinematic preview is never marketed as playable gameplay. A teaser is never marketed as a full film. No claims of published episodes without actual public URLs.

## Release formats
- **Sicario — Dead Man Walking**: short, coherent cinematic chapters, original illustrated comic issues and eventually a season/feature compilation. Maintain Don Azrael continuity, original approved opening and sound direction, versioned canonical characters and story bible; no unlicensed real-person cameos.
- **Badman Kombat (BMK)**: first **playable** episode as one polished arena/fight with controls, hit detection, win/lose and supported-device test; subsequent episodes add fighters/styles/locations only after quality proof. Preserve Jamaica early-1980s setting and lore; avoid presenting cutscene as working game.
- **Other JingWu Media universes**: episodic film/comic/game instalments as appropriate to original assets; use story arcs and clear episode numbers. Preserve project-specific canon and approved masters.


## Transmedia episodes — cartoons, comics, and podcasts
A single **canonical story episode** can be adapted into different finished media without forcing a one-to-one literal copy:
- **Comic issue:** sequential original art and lettering, downloadable/mobile-readable comic, distinct issue number, panel continuity, accessible text alternative where feasible.
- **Cartoon/animation:** animated short using authorised character designs, consistent voices/appearance, motion QA and legible captions. Do not call a slideshow full animation.
- **Audio drama / comic-book podcast:** adapt the issue into a fully understandable **audio-first narrative** with narrator, character dialogue, approved original music and licensed/original sound effects. Listeners should not need to see the panels to follow the plot.
- **Creator commentary podcast:** a separately labelled behind-the-scenes discussion about writing, characters and process; do not misrepresent it as dramatic fiction.
- **Playable chapter:** only where gameplay is real and independently tested; share canon while preserving game-specific choices.

### Comic-to-podcast production workflow
1. Select one finished, rights-cleared comic issue and lock its script/canonical scene order (CIT: preserve approved dialogue and character identity).
2. Write an audio adaptation: describe visually essential actions in natural narrative, not by reading every panel caption verbatim. Preserve the plot and intentions; label any approved additions.
3. Record voices with permission (including any AI voice-cloning consent), separate stems for narration, dialogue, score and Foley, and log rights; avoid unauthorised celebrity voice impersonations.
4. Mix and review on headphones and phone speakers for speech clarity, sensible loudness, intentional sound effects, and no duplicated audio; apply the Cinematic Sound Standard.
5. QA start-to-finish, export audio with title, episode number, credits, rights, captions/transcript, cover art and preview. Confirm any distributor/podcast host actually accepts the format.
6. Publish only to verified accounts with actual episode link and delivery/checkout evidence. Preserve separate master files and hashes for each format.

### Episode identity and cross-format catalog
Give each story a stable identifier such as `SIC-S01E01` and distinct format revisions `-COMIC-v1`, `-AUDIO-v1`, `-ANIM-v1`. Store one story bible and linked derivative asset manifests; maintain clear release status **PLANNED / IN_PRODUCTION / QA_PASSED / RELEASED_VERIFIED** independently for each format. An audio release does not imply the cartoon or comic has shipped. Avoid revealing unlicensed source or unreleased private assets.

### Revenue experiment
Offer a genuine free sample/trailer, then test individual comic issues, audio chapters, optional bundles and collector editions through platform-compliant checkout. Podcast feed rules, music rights, accessibility, delivery cost, consumer refunds, VAT and paid-feed entitlements must be confirmed first. Never assume a new episode will sell or treat followers/streams as purchases.

### First practical cross-format test
Assess **Sicario** comic issue 1 as a candidate; when the approved script and artwork are actually available, produce a short audio-drama proof from one complete scene, then compare its completion/engagement with a comic sample before promising a full season. BMK can also use narrated fighter origin episodes, while preserving the 1980s Kingston canon. This is a scoped conversion of existing projects, not permission to open additional production lanes.

## Episode production contract
Every episode record must include:
1. Series title, season/episode identifier, version, source assets and checksum/provenance.
2. One-sentence audience payoff; standalone story arc; precisely scoped runtime/playability; continuity references.
3. Deliverable proof (playable build or watchable video/PDF), aspect/audio/mobile QA, rights/likeness review.
4. Audience discovery clip with original/licensed visuals, captions and truthful stage.
5. Sales route, price/entitlement (if any), platform-eligible checkout, verified delivery/receipt/refund and support.
6. Actual published link, release timestamp, customer response, net revenue and defects. Unknown values stay unknown.
7. Optional next-episode hook and replay/archive path. Game save compatibility across episodes required before charging for sequels.

## Commercial experiments
Launch Episode 1 free when an actual usable sample exists; validate attention and completion, then test affordable paid follow-on episodes or a complete-season collection where legal/platform terms allow. Do not presuppose fans will pay, invent bookings, or commit release dates without validated masters. Keep existing Payhip for eligible off-platform sales, Telegram Stars for qualifying in-bot digital purchases, and x402 only for compliant AI service endpoints.

## Allocation and gates
This strategy **does not override ATLAS's freeze or the three active release lanes** in ops/RELEASE_FACTORY_CONTROL.md. It reduces existing product scope into a testable, releasable unit, not permission to start more unfinished projects. AUREUS scores chapters in the portfolio engine against evidence and time-to-release; ATLAS picks one current chapter based on recoverable assets, QA, rights and monetisation path; CIT and Continuity Guard enforce 'improve without destroying approved work'. Release decisions require evidence rather than fictional progress.

## First candidate assessment (not a release claim)
- Sicario: inventory actual approved media and find an independently watchable coherent chapter before promising episode 1.
- BMK: verify existing source and whether a complete one-fight vertical slice can be played. If not, classify footage as cinematic proof-of-concept.
- Initial project revenue: compare a saleable digital comic instalment against software with tested checkout; do not neglect verified near-sale products solely for episodic novelty.
